CLINICAL GUIDELINES — St. Mary's Private Hospital

Metformin Usage Guidelines:
Metformin is strictly contraindicated in patients with severe renal failure (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73m2).
Risk of lactic acidosis increases significantly with renal impairment and can be life-threatening.
For patients with mild to moderate kidney disease (eGFR 30-60), metformin may be used with dose reduction and close monitoring.
Metformin should be discontinued if eGFR drops below 30 at any point during treatment.

Metformin Dosing:
Standard starting dose is 500mg once or twice daily with meals.
Dose should be increased gradually over several weeks based on tolerance.
Maximum recommended daily dose is 2000mg to 2550mg depending on formulation.
Starting directly at 2000mg is not recommended and may cause severe gastrointestinal side effects.

Ibuprofen Usage Guidelines:
Maximum safe dose of ibuprofen for adults is 400mg per dose, up to 1200mg per day for over-the-counter use.
Ibuprofen should always be taken with food or milk to reduce risk of gastric irritation.
Long-term use increases risk of gastric bleeding and ulcers.
Ibuprofen is contraindicated in patients with active peptic ulcers or severe renal impairment.

Blood Sugar Guidelines:
Normal fasting blood glucose is below 100 mg/dL.
Prediabetes is diagnosed when fasting glucose is between 100 and 125 mg/dL.
Diabetes is diagnosed when fasting glucose is 126 mg/dL or above on two separate tests.
A single fasting blood sugar of 200 mg/dL or above with symptoms may also indicate diabetes.
