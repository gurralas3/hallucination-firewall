Non-Compete Agreements in California:
California Business and Professions Code Section 16600 states that every contract by which anyone is restrained from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is void.
Non-compete agreements are completely banned and unenforceable in California, with very limited exceptions.
California is one of only a few states that imposes a near-total ban on non-compete agreements.
Employers cannot require employees to sign non-compete agreements as a condition of employment in California.
Even if a non-compete agreement is signed, it cannot be enforced in California courts.
There is no valid duration for a non-compete in California — not 1 year, not 2 years, not any duration.

Non-Disclosure Agreements in California:
Non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) protecting genuine trade secrets are generally enforceable in California.
NDAs cannot be used to prevent employees from disclosing illegal conduct, harassment, or discrimination.
Senate Bill 331 restricts use of NDAs to silence employees about workplace misconduct.

Employment At-Will in California:
California is an at-will employment state, meaning either party can terminate employment at any time without cause.
Exceptions include termination that violates public policy, implied contracts, and the implied covenant of good faith.

Minimum Wage in California:
California state minimum wage is $16.00 per hour as of January 2024.
Some cities and counties have higher local minimum wage requirements.
