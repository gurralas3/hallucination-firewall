MEDICINE: Amoxicillin
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: Bacterial infections — pneumonia, ear infections, urinary tract infections, skin infections, tonsillitis.
ADULT DOSE: 250–500mg every 8 hours orally for 5–7 days. Severe: 875mg every 12 hours.
CHILD DOSE (under 5, pneumonia): 40mg/kg/day in two divided doses for 5 days.
CHILD DOSE (general, by weight): 8kg = 160mg twice daily. 10kg = 200mg twice daily. 15kg = 300mg twice daily. 20kg = 400mg twice daily.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Penicillin allergy. Caution in mononucleosis.
NOTES: First-line WHO treatment for childhood pneumonia. Take full course even if feeling better.

MEDICINE: Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Co-Amoxiclav)
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: Infections resistant to plain Amoxicillin — sinusitis, diabetic foot, animal bites, skin abscesses.
ADULT DOSE: 625mg every 8 hours or 1g every 12 hours for 5–7 days.
CHILD DOSE: 40mg/kg/day of amoxicillin component in divided doses every 8 hours.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Penicillin allergy. Liver disease. Prior jaundice from this medicine.
NOTES: Stronger than plain Amoxicillin. Higher GI side effects — take with food.

MEDICINE: Doxycycline
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: Respiratory infections, sexually transmitted infections, malaria prophylaxis, Lyme disease, rickettsial disease.
ADULT DOSE: 100mg twice daily for 7 days. Malaria prophylaxis: 100mg daily starting 1–2 days before travel.
CHILD DOSE: Not recommended under 8 years — causes permanent tooth discolouration.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Children under 8 years. Pregnancy. Breastfeeding.
NOTES: Take with full glass of water. Do not lie down for 30 minutes after dose. Avoid sun exposure.

MEDICINE: Ciprofloxacin
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: Urinary tract infections, gastrointestinal infections, respiratory infections, anthrax exposure.
ADULT DOSE: 250–500mg twice daily for 3–7 days depending on indication.
CHILD DOSE: Not routinely recommended in children — use only under medical supervision. 10–15mg/kg twice daily if essential.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Hypersensitivity to fluoroquinolones. Caution in children and adolescents — affects cartilage development.
NOTES: Not first-line for simple UTI. Avoid antacids within 2 hours of dose.

MEDICINE: Metronidazole
CATEGORY: Antibiotic / Antiprotozoal
INDICATIONS: Anaerobic bacterial infections, giardiasis, amoebiasis, trichomoniasis, bacterial vaginosis.
ADULT DOSE: 400–500mg three times daily for 5–7 days. Giardia: 2g single dose.
CHILD DOSE: 7.5mg/kg three times daily for 5–7 days.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: First trimester of pregnancy. Alcohol — causes severe vomiting reaction.
NOTES: Do not drink alcohol during treatment and for 48 hours after. Metallic taste is common.

MEDICINE: Cotrimoxazole (Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole)
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: UTI, PCP pneumonia in HIV patients, daily HIV prophylaxis in children.
ADULT DOSE: 960mg twice daily for UTI (3–5 days). PCP prophylaxis: 960mg daily.
CHILD DOSE: 24mg/kg/day in two divided doses. HIV prophylaxis: 240mg daily from 6 weeks of age.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Sulfonamide allergy. Severe kidney or liver impairment. Neonates under 6 weeks.
NOTES: Essential for HIV-positive children as daily prophylaxis. Do not use in newborns under 6 weeks.

MEDICINE: Gentamicin
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: Severe bacterial infections, neonatal sepsis, pyelonephritis, endocarditis.
ADULT DOSE: 3–5mg/kg/day IV or IM once daily.
CHILD DOSE (neonates, first week): 5mg/kg every 36 hours.
CHILD DOSE (infants and children): 7.5mg/kg once daily.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Kidney impairment. History of hearing loss from aminoglycosides.
NOTES: Monitor kidney function and hearing with prolonged use. Risk of permanent hearing loss at high doses.

MEDICINE: Benzylpenicillin (Penicillin G)
CATEGORY: Antibiotic
INDICATIONS: Severe streptococcal infections, meningitis, syphilis, anthrax, neonatal infections.
ADULT DOSE: 1.2–2.4g IV every 4–6 hours depending on severity.
CHILD DOSE (neonatal meningitis): 50mg/kg every 12 hours for first week, then every 8 hours.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Penicillin allergy.
NOTES: IV or IM only — not for oral use. First-line for neonatal sepsis in resource-limited settings.

MEDICINE: Artemether-Lumefantrine (Coartem)
CATEGORY: Antimalarial
INDICATIONS: Uncomplicated Plasmodium falciparum malaria.
ADULT DOSE: 4 tablets at 0h, 8h, 24h, 36h, 48h, 60h (6 doses total over 3 days).
CHILD DOSE (5–14kg): 1 tablet per dose × 6 doses.
CHILD DOSE (15–24kg): 2 tablets per dose × 6 doses.
CHILD DOSE (25–34kg): 3 tablets per dose × 6 doses.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: First trimester pregnancy. Severe malaria (use IV Artesunate instead). Cardiac arrhythmia.
NOTES: Must be taken with food or milk fat for absorption. Not for severe malaria.

MEDICINE: Artesunate (IV)
CATEGORY: Antimalarial
INDICATIONS: Severe Plasmodium falciparum malaria — the only WHO-recommended treatment for severe malaria.
ADULT DOSE: 2.4mg/kg IV at 0h, 12h, 24h, then once daily.
CHILD DOSE (under 20kg): 3mg/kg IV at same intervals.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Known hypersensitivity to artemisinins.
NOTES: Replaces quinine as first-line for severe malaria per WHO 2023 guidelines. Oral treatment must follow IV course.

MEDICINE: Chloroquine
CATEGORY: Antimalarial
INDICATIONS: Plasmodium vivax and P. ovale malaria (NOT for P. falciparum in most regions — resistant).
ADULT DOSE: 600mg base on day 1, 300mg on day 2, 300mg on day 3.
CHILD DOSE: 10mg base/kg on day 1, 5mg/kg on days 2 and 3.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Known chloroquine resistance in region. Retinal disease. Epilepsy.
NOTES: Not effective against P. falciparum in most of Africa, Asia, South America — confirm local resistance patterns.

MEDICINE: Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)
CATEGORY: Analgesic / Antipyretic
INDICATIONS: Mild to moderate pain, fever.
ADULT DOSE: 500–1000mg every 4–6 hours. Maximum 4000mg per day (4g).
CHILD DOSE: 10–15mg/kg every 4–6 hours. Maximum 5 doses per 24 hours. Do not exceed 60mg/kg/day.
CHILD DOSE (by weight): 5kg = 75mg. 10kg = 150mg. 15kg = 225mg. 20kg = 300mg. Per dose.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Severe liver disease. Do not combine with other paracetamol-containing products.
NOTES: Most common cause of acute liver failure in overdose. Never exceed recommended dose.

MEDICINE: Ibuprofen
CATEGORY: NSAID / Analgesic
INDICATIONS: Mild to moderate pain, fever, inflammation.
ADULT DOSE: 200–400mg every 4–6 hours. Maximum 1200mg per day (OTC). Up to 2400mg/day under supervision.
CHILD DOSE (over 6 months): 5–10mg/kg every 6–8 hours. Maximum 30mg/kg/day.
CHILD DOSE: NOT recommended under 6 months.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Under 6 months. Peptic ulcer. Kidney impairment. Aspirin allergy. Third trimester pregnancy.
NOTES: Take with food to reduce stomach irritation. Avoid in dehydrated children — risk of kidney injury.

MEDICINE: Aspirin
CATEGORY: Analgesic / Antiplatelet
INDICATIONS: Pain, fever (adults only). Antiplatelet: post-MI, stroke prevention, cardiovascular disease.
ADULT DOSE: Pain/fever: 300–600mg every 4–6 hours. Antiplatelet: 75–100mg once daily.
CHILD DOSE: NOT recommended under 16 years — risk of Reye's syndrome (fatal liver and brain damage).
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Children under 16. Peptic ulcer. Bleeding disorders. Anticoagulant therapy.
NOTES: Never give to children or teenagers with viral illness. Critical contraindication.

MEDICINE: Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)
CATEGORY: Rehydration
INDICATIONS: Dehydration from diarrhoea and vomiting in all age groups.
ADULT DOSE: 200–400ml after each loose stool until diarrhoea stops.
CHILD DOSE (under 2 years): 50–100ml after each stool.
CHILD DOSE (2–10 years): 100–200ml after each stool.
CHILD DOSE (over 10 years): as much as wanted.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Severe dehydration requiring IV fluids. Unconscious patient.
NOTES: First-line treatment for diarrhoeal dehydration. Do not add extra sugar or salt. Use safe water.

MEDICINE: Zinc Sulfate
CATEGORY: Nutritional / Antidiarrhoeal
INDICATIONS: Adjunct treatment for childhood diarrhoea. Zinc deficiency.
CHILD DOSE (under 6 months): 10mg daily for 10–14 days.
CHILD DOSE (6 months and above): 20mg daily for 10–14 days.
ADULT DOSE: 20mg daily for zinc deficiency.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: None significant at therapeutic doses.
NOTES: Always give alongside ORS for childhood diarrhoea. Reduces duration and severity.

MEDICINE: Iron (Ferrous Sulfate)
CATEGORY: Nutritional
INDICATIONS: Iron-deficiency anaemia. Anaemia in pregnancy.
ADULT DOSE: 200mg (65mg elemental iron) two to three times daily.
CHILD DOSE: 3–6mg/kg/day of elemental iron in divided doses.
PREGNANT WOMEN: 60mg elemental iron daily for prevention. 120mg daily for treatment.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Haemochromatosis. Haemolytic anaemia. Repeated blood transfusions.
NOTES: Take on empty stomach if tolerated — increases absorption. Turns stools dark — normal.

MEDICINE: Folic Acid
CATEGORY: Nutritional
INDICATIONS: Prevention of neural tube defects. Megaloblastic anaemia. Supplement in pregnancy.
ADULT DOSE: Anaemia: 5mg daily for 4 months. Pregnancy prevention: 0.4mg daily before and during first trimester.
CHILD DOSE: 5mg daily for megaloblastic anaemia.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Undiagnosed anaemia — can mask B12 deficiency.
NOTES: All women of childbearing age should take 0.4mg folic acid daily ideally before conception.

MEDICINE: Vitamin A
CATEGORY: Nutritional
INDICATIONS: Vitamin A deficiency. Prevention of complications in measles. Night blindness.
CHILD DOSE (under 6 months): 50,000 IU once.
CHILD DOSE (6–11 months): 100,000 IU once.
CHILD DOSE (12 months and above): 200,000 IU once, repeat after 4 weeks in deficiency.
ADULT DOSE: 200,000 IU for deficiency.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Hypervitaminosis A. Pregnancy — high doses teratogenic.
NOTES: Give immediately to children with measles in areas of deficiency. Reduces measles mortality.

MEDICINE: Metformin
CATEGORY: Antidiabetic
INDICATIONS: Type 2 diabetes mellitus. Not for Type 1 diabetes.
ADULT DOSE: Start 500mg twice daily with meals. Increase to 1000–2000mg/day over 4 weeks.
CHILD DOSE: Not recommended under 10 years. Age 10+: 500mg twice daily, maximum 2000mg/day.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Kidney impairment (eGFR below 30). Liver disease. Heart failure. Alcohol excess.
NOTES: Take with food. Monitor kidney function annually. Causes lactic acidosis in kidney failure.

MEDICINE: Glibenclamide (Glyburide)
CATEGORY: Antidiabetic
INDICATIONS: Type 2 diabetes when Metformin insufficient or not tolerated.
ADULT DOSE: 2.5–5mg once daily with breakfast. Maximum 15mg/day.
CHILD DOSE: Not recommended.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Type 1 diabetes. Severe kidney or liver disease. Pregnancy (use insulin instead).
NOTES: Risk of hypoglycaemia — monitor blood glucose. Elderly patients particularly at risk.

MEDICINE: Insulin (Regular / NPH)
CATEGORY: Antidiabetic
INDICATIONS: Type 1 diabetes. Type 2 diabetes not controlled by oral agents. Gestational diabetes. Diabetic emergencies.
ADULT DOSE: Highly individualised — guided by blood glucose monitoring. Typically 0.5–1 unit/kg/day total.
CHILD DOSE: 0.5–1 unit/kg/day, adjusted by blood glucose.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Hypoglycaemia (current episode).
NOTES: Store in refrigerator. Never freeze. Opened vials stable at room temperature for 28 days.

MEDICINE: Amlodipine
CATEGORY: Antihypertensive (Calcium Channel Blocker)
INDICATIONS: Hypertension. Stable angina.
ADULT DOSE: 5mg once daily. Increase to 10mg if needed after 4 weeks.
CHILD DOSE (6–17 years): 2.5–5mg once daily.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Severe hypotension. Cardiogenic shock. Unstable angina.
NOTES: Long-acting — once daily dosing. Ankle swelling common. Do not stop abruptly.

MEDICINE: Lisinopril
CATEGORY: Antihypertensive (ACE Inhibitor)
INDICATIONS: Hypertension. Heart failure. Diabetic nephropathy.
ADULT DOSE: 5–10mg once daily. Maintenance 10–40mg/day.
CHILD DOSE (6 years and above): 0.07mg/kg once daily, maximum 5mg initially.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Pregnancy (teratogenic). Angioedema history. Bilateral renal artery stenosis. High potassium.
NOTES: Dry cough in 10–15% of patients — switch to ARB if intolerable. Monitor kidney function and potassium.

MEDICINE: Hydrochlorothiazide
CATEGORY: Antihypertensive (Diuretic)
INDICATIONS: Hypertension. Oedema in heart failure.
ADULT DOSE: 12.5–25mg once daily in the morning.
CHILD DOSE: 1–2mg/kg/day in one or two doses.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Anuria. Severe kidney impairment. Sulfonamide allergy.
NOTES: Take in morning to avoid night-time urination. Monitor potassium — can cause hypokalaemia.

MEDICINE: Salbutamol (Albuterol)
CATEGORY: Bronchodilator
INDICATIONS: Acute asthma attack. Bronchospasm. COPD exacerbation.
ADULT DOSE: Inhaler: 100–200mcg (1–2 puffs) every 4–6 hours as needed. Nebuliser: 2.5mg every 20 minutes for acute attack.
CHILD DOSE: Inhaler: 100mcg (1 puff) every 4–6 hours. Nebuliser: 2.5mg (under 5 years), every 20 minutes in acute attack.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Known hypersensitivity. Use with caution in cardiac arrhythmia.
NOTES: Reliever — not preventer. Overuse indicates poor asthma control. Shake inhaler before use.

MEDICINE: Prednisolone
CATEGORY: Corticosteroid
INDICATIONS: Severe asthma, allergic reactions, inflammatory conditions, adrenal insufficiency.
ADULT DOSE: Severe asthma: 40–50mg once daily for 5 days. Anti-inflammatory: 5–60mg/day depending on condition.
CHILD DOSE (asthma): 1–2mg/kg/day (maximum 40mg) for 3–5 days.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Systemic fungal infection. Live vaccines during high-dose therapy.
NOTES: Short courses (under 2 weeks) — can stop without tapering. Long courses require gradual reduction to avoid adrenal crisis.

MEDICINE: Oxytocin
CATEGORY: Uterotonic
INDICATIONS: Prevention and treatment of postpartum haemorrhage (PPH). Induction of labour.
PPH PREVENTION DOSE: 10 IU intramuscularly immediately after delivery of baby.
PPH TREATMENT DOSE: 20–40 IU in 1 litre IV fluid, infuse at rate to control bleeding.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Uterine hyperstimulation. Fetal distress before delivery.
NOTES: First-line drug for PPH prevention. Most critical medicine in preventing maternal death. Keep refrigerated.

MEDICINE: Misoprostol
CATEGORY: Uterotonic
INDICATIONS: Prevention of PPH when oxytocin unavailable. Medical management of incomplete abortion.
PPH PREVENTION DOSE: 600mcg sublingually immediately after delivery — use when oxytocin not available.
INCOMPLETE ABORTION: 400–800mcg sublingually or vaginally.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Prior caesarean section (risk of uterine rupture with induction). Allergy to prostaglandins.
NOTES: Heat-stable alternative to oxytocin — critical for rural settings without refrigeration. Causes shivering and fever — normal.

MEDICINE: Magnesium Sulfate
CATEGORY: Anticonvulsant / Uterotonic
INDICATIONS: Prevention and treatment of eclampsia. Severe pre-eclampsia.
LOADING DOSE: 4g IV slowly over 5–20 minutes, PLUS 5g IM in each buttock (10g IM total).
MAINTENANCE DOSE: 5g IM every 4 hours OR 1–2g/hour IV infusion.
STOP IF: Respiratory rate below 16/min. Absent patellar reflex. Urine output below 25ml/hour.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Respiratory failure. Absent tendon reflexes. Severe kidney impairment.
NOTES: Antidote is Calcium Gluconate 1g IV — always have available. First-line for eclampsia globally.

MEDICINE: Chlorhexidine (7.1% solution)
CATEGORY: Antiseptic
INDICATIONS: Umbilical cord care in newborns to prevent omphalitis and neonatal sepsis.
DOSE: Apply once daily to umbilical stump for 7 days after birth.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Known allergy to chlorhexidine.
NOTES: WHO-recommended for cord care in high neonatal mortality settings. Reduces neonatal deaths from cord infection.

MEDICINE: Diazepam
CATEGORY: Anticonvulsant / Anxiolytic
INDICATIONS: Seizures, status epilepticus, severe anxiety, alcohol withdrawal, muscle spasm.
ADULT DOSE (seizures): 5–10mg IV slowly. Repeat after 10–15 minutes if needed. Maximum 30mg.
CHILD DOSE (seizures): 0.3mg/kg IV or 0.5mg/kg rectally. Maximum 10mg single dose.
CONTRAINDICATIONS: Respiratory depression. Myasthenia gravis. Severe liver disease. First trimester pregnancy.

MEDICINE: Phenobarbital
CATEGORY: Anticonvulsant
INDICATIONS: Epilepsy. Neonatal seizures. Status epilepticus (second-line).

CHILD DOSE (neonates): 20mg/kg IV loading dose. Maintenance: 3–5mg/kg/day.

NOTES: Enzyme inducer — reduces effect of many drugs including contraceptives and antiretrovirals. Sedating.

MEDICINE: Amitriptyline
CATEGORY: Antidepressant / Neuropathic pain

MEDICINE: Haloperidol
CATEGORY: Antipsychotic

MEDICINE: Fluconazole

MEDICINE: Albendazole

MEDICINE: Mebendazole

MEDICINE: Aciclovir (Acyclovir)

MEDICINE: Zidovudine (AZT)

MEDICINE: Lamivudine (3TC)

MEDICINE: Ketotifen (Eye Drops)

MEDICINE: Tetrahydrozoline (Visine / Eye Drops)

MEDICINE: Sodium Cromoglicate (Eye Drops)

CHILD DOSE (over 2 years): Same as adult.
